Going wide, and going deep too

The wide videos are working. I would push them further, and give some of them a deep side, so the channel grows toward 1M without losing what built it.

I see the channel from the outside. The team knows its numbers and its reasons, and I may be missing context that changes all of this.

What I see

Over the last few months the channel has published more often, and more of the videos are wide and beginner-friendly: careers and how to learn. That is working. Ansh has said that reaching 1 million subscribers is one of the two things he cares about most right now, and wide videos are how a channel gets there.

I would push even more in that direction. But the reputation of Cutting Edge was built on the long, deep courses, the ones people saved and came back to. Those are rarer now. A new viewer who arrives through a wide video may never see that side of the channel.

The idea

Keep the wide video as it is, and give it a deep companion. For each wide video, a research intern prepares one page that goes further:

  • Sources: the papers, posts and launches behind the claims in the video.
  • Prompts: the exact prompts to try what the video shows.
  • Tool comparison: the two or three tools that do the job, tested side by side.
  • Next steps: what to learn or build after watching.

The video stays light and quick to make. The viewer who wants more gets more, and the channel keeps its name for depth. None of this slows down the publishing pace, because the companion is the intern's work, not the editors'.

The reference I have in mind is Greg Isenberg: broad, easy-to-watch episodes, with deeper material for the people who want to go further.

A question

The channel used to run an AI news series, and I have not seen a new episode in a while. Would a short weekly AI news brief, researched by the intern, be useful? I do not know why the series paused, so I am asking rather than suggesting.
